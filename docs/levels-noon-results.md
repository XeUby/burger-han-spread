1. Keep the moderate payment/first-option correction as the recorded lower-level deployment choice. It improved the two-week development comparisons at levels1–3 and all ranked results remain visible here. It is not a statistically promoted universal policy.
2. Do not transfer the strong0.72payment reduction indiscriminately. It lost profit at levels1–2 even after the option fix. At level3bundling helped, but moderate payment earned more with better timeliness. Driver learning, route formation and supply regime affect the result.
3. Do not deploy the global portfolio model at lower levels: its conservative cost assumptions excluded too much profitable work. At level8it earned a modest development gain, below the declared promotion screen. Solver correctness does not validate the supplied economics.
4. The matched ranking gaps remain real. Reliable qualification and improvement over our own control do not establish parity with the leaders. Admission, route opportunities and actual booking-cost response need further level-specific investigation.
5. The requested pause before levels 5–8 was subsequently lifted by explicit user authorization. The separately frozen upper-level policy retained the level 8 control; the operational failure and incomplete upper submission are recorded in levels-upper-results.md.
